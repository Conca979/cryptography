\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Problem Statement: Automated Image Scraping and Economic Threats}
Images are widely stored and distributed via websites and Content Delivery Networks (CDNs), which enable images to be loaded and collected automatically at low processing cost \cite{uzun2023scraping}. This may allow unauthorized and illegal redistribution of images \cite{shan2023glaze}. In addition, existing image-protection methods may involve a trade-off between protection and performance. In pixel-chaotic ciphers, shuffling individual pixels across the entire image destroys spatial correlation, leading to an increase in compressed file size. Therefore, websites need to balance image protection, bandwidth costs, and display performance.

\subsection{Project Objectives}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Adapt and implement GRT (Generalized Rectangular Transform)-based tile-level image scrambling on the server and client-side viewport reconstruction (Section~\ref{sec:architecture}).
    \item Validate the reversibility of GRT and confirm pixel-perfect image recovery with no quality loss (Section~\ref{sec:GRT}).
    \item Measure computational latency, memory usage, and compression overhead across standard web formats (PNG, WebP, JPEG) to assess its feasibility for web-based image protection (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark_analysis}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Background}
\subsection{From SRT to GRT}
\label{sec:GRT}

Special Rectangular Transform (SRT) is a transformation method used to rearrange pixel positions, allowing accurate image reconstruction through its corresponding inverse transformation (ISRT) \cite{Lan2025ANM}. The SRT matrix is expressed as:
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{SRT}} =
\begin{bmatrix}
        1 & r \\
        s & rs+1
    \end{bmatrix}
\label{eq:srt}
\end{equation}
\noindent where \(r\) and \(s\) are two integer parameters that determine the transformation matrix. However, its transformation matrix is constrained because the upper-left element is fixed at 1, resulting in only two degrees of freedom. To address this limitation, we adopt the GRT to enhance the flexibility of the transformation matrix and introduces a translation offset $(r_m, r_n)$ to displace the origin (Figure~\ref{fig:grt_displaced}).



Unlike SRT, GRT involves 4 parameters \((a,b,c,d)\), providing 4 degrees of freedom and greater flexibility in the transformation matrix \cite{Lan2025ANM}:

\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}_{n+1} = 
\left\{ 
    \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} 
    \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}_n + 
    \begin{bmatrix} r_m \\ r_n \end{bmatrix} 
\right\} \bmod 
\begin{bmatrix} M \\ N \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
\label{eq:grt}

\noindent where \((a,b,c,d)\) are 4 transformation parameters, $G = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ is a general $2 \times 2$ integer matrix whose determinant $\|G\|$ does not need to equal $1$, \((x, y)_n\) are original pixel coordinates, \((x, y)_{n+1}\) are transformed tile coordinates, \((r_m, r_n)\) are coordinate shifting offsets (Figure~\ref{fig:srt_vs_grt}), and \(M\), \(N\) are image height and width. However, the parameter set \((a,b,c,d)\) must satisfy these 4 mathematical conditions \cite{Lan2025ANM}. 

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Condition 1 --- $(b \bmod h = 0)$ or $(c \bmod v = 0)$.}
    \item \textbf{Condition 2 --- $\gcd(\|G\|, p) = 1$.}
    \item \textbf{Condition 3 --- $\gcd(a, h) = 1$.}
    \item \textbf{Condition 4 --- $\gcd(d, v) = 1$.}
\end{enumerate}
\label{4_conditions}

\noindent where $p = \gcd(M, N)$ is the greatest common divisor of the grid dimensions, and $h = M/p, v = N/p$ represent the irreducible aspect ratio. These conditions ensure a one-to-one mapping across rectangular grids without tile collisions.

\subsection{Image decryption via Inverse GRT (IGRT)}
\label{sec:IGRT}

\subsection{Key Terminology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pixel Permutation} rearranges pixel positions without changing their values. In GRT, a flexible transformation matrix is used to disrupt spatial correlations between neighboring pixels \cite{Lan2025ANM}.
    \item \textbf{Tile-Based Scrambling} splits the image into several tiles and then permutes their positions. The original image can be reconstructed by restoring the tiles to their original positions.
    \item \textbf{Reversibility} allows the reconstruction of the original image, while \textbf{Bijective Mapping} ensures ``one-to-one" correspondence between original and transformed positions.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Architecture \& Application}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{Secret Delivery}
    To protect web images, we propose breaking the pipeline as a symmetric permutation cryptosystem $(\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{C}, \mathcal{K}, \mathrm{Enc}_K, \mathrm{Dec}_K)$ operating over a two-channel delivery protocol (Figure~\ref{fig:system_architecture}).

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/system_architecture_compacted.drawio.pdf}
    \caption{Cryptographic delivery across server, network, and client.}
    \label{fig:system_architecture}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Channel A (Public CDN Asset):} Where automated bots, \texttt{curl} scripts, or crawlers fetching the image URL directly would receive only an unusable grid of shuffled tiles.
    \item \textbf{Channel B (Side-Channel for Key Delivery):} Delivers the secret $K = (M, N, a, b, c, d, r_m, r_n)$ and original dimensions $(W_0, H_0)$ separately to the authorized active browser session.
\end{itemize}

% (\texttt{igrt\_client.js})

\subsection{Encryption \& Decryption Lifecycle (Figure~\ref{fig:system_architecture})}
The  cryptographic pipeline executes five stages across the server and client:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Padding:} If the source image dimensions $(W_0, H_0)$ are not divisible by $(M, N)$, the server will pad the outer edges until every tile is identical in size.
    \item \textbf{Key Generation:} The server generates a secret key $K = (a, b, c, d, r_m, r_n)$ satisfying \hyperref[4_conditions]{4 conditions}.
    \item \textbf{Encryption (Forward GRT):} Plaintext tiles at $(x, y)_n$ is mapped to its ciphertext coordinate $(x, y)_{n+1}$ via the affine transformation in Eq.~(\hyperref[eq:grt]{2}).
    \item \textbf{Decryption Precomputation:} Upon receiving secret key $K$, the client evaluates the 4-stage IGRT corollary (Figure~\ref{fig:igrt_4stages}) once per axis, storing the inverse mapping in 1D lookup tables in $\mathcal{O}(M+N)$ time.
    \item \textbf{Decryption (Plaintext Recovery):} The client applies the inverse permutation to restore all tiles to their original coordinates and crops the image back to $(W_0, H_0)$, satisfying $\mathrm{Dec}_K(\mathrm{Enc}_K(P)) = P$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Decryption Recovery Analysis}

A valid symmetric cipher must be strictly invertible: decrypting a ciphertext with the correct key must return the exact original plaintext. We evaluated our implementation across three standard resolutions:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}

    \item \textbf{SD 480p ($480 \times 854$, padded to $480 \times 875$):} $M \times N = 30 \times 25$ grid ($750$ tiles of $16 \times 35\text{ px}$).
    
    \item \textbf{HD 720p ($720 \times 1280$):} $M \times N = 36 \times 40$ grid ($1440$ tiles of $20 \times 32\text{ px}$).
    
    \item \textbf{FHD 1080p ($1920 \times 1080$):} $M \times N = 40 \times 40$ grid ($1600$ tiles of $48 \times 27\text{ px}$).
\end{enumerate}


As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:t5_verification}, comparing the original plaintext $P$ and decrypted output $\mathrm{Dec}_K(\mathrm{Enc}_K(P))$ results in zero mismatched pixels and a Mean Squared Error of $\text{MSE} = 0.0$, confirming $100\%$ bit-exact recovery.

\noindent \textbf{Why Tile Transposition Preserves Compression as We Expected:} Shuffling individual pixels will turn an image into random-looking noise that image compressors cannot shrink. By permuting
whole tiles (16 × 35 up to 48 × 27 px), pixels inside each tile stay together. Only the edges between adjacent tiles change, keeping the expansion between all three resolutions $< 7\%$.

\section{Conclusion}
By adapting the Generalized Rectangular Transform (\hyperref[sec:GRT]{GRT}) and its 4-stage inverse (\hyperref[sec:IGRT]{IGRT})~\cite{Lan2025ANM} from pixel-level encryption to tile-level web delivery, our system prevents automated HTTP image scraping while keeping compression overhead low. With the client-side reconstruction under $2.72\text{ ms}$, show that our system can operate at production speeds in web browsers.
